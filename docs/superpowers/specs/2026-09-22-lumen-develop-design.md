# Lumen — Develop, simple edits, labels and metadata-faithful export (Phase 8) Design

**Status:** approved 2026-09-22 (sections 1 and 1-revised approved in conversation; 2–4 follow the approaches chosen: CPU pipeline in Rust, Auto as a measured-then-searched ML optimisation, denoise on demand) · **Spine:** `2026-09-17-photos-app-design.md` §2 (non-goals amended by this document), §12 (edits)

## Why

Lightroom exports lose metadata; the user re-added it by hand to every edited photo. Lumen gets a proper developer — sliders, curves, colour, ML Auto and denoise, for RAW and for JPEG/PNG/HEIC/WebP/TIFF alike — whose exports carry **all** the original's metadata into whatever format they are written as, and prove it by reading it back. The existing viewer editor becomes the quick one: geometry, and trimming for video. Colour labels and flags come with it, so a shoot can be culled before it is developed.

**Safety is the first requirement:** originals are never written, opened for writing, or moved. Edits are recipes; derived images are caches; an export never overwrites an existing file.

## 1. Screens (approved)

**Simple editor** (viewer, `?asset=<id>&edit=1`): crop with aspect presets, rotate 90°, straighten, flip horizontal and vertical. For a video it is a **trimmer**: in/out points and any number of cut-out segments over a filmstrip of the clip, previewed as the cut timeline. The tone sliders it had move to Develop.

*Revised 2026-09-22:* no side column. The picture fills the viewer and the tools float in a toolbar over its lower edge; Undo / Redo (`Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z`) sit in the top bar with Revert, Cancel and Done. Geometry is drawn in the webview with CSS transforms laid out in the renderer's order (Rust renders the photo once without geometry), so turns, flips and leaving crop mode **animate** and cost no render. Guides: thirds or a fine grid (*Auto*: the fine grid while straightening), an exact-angle field, and the crop's size in the original's pixels. Flips act on what is on screen and a turn carries the crop with it. The trimmer's controls sit under its timeline. Every shortcut is listed in **Settings → Keyboard**.

**Develop** (`/develop`, `/develop/:id`; a sidebar entry under Import; *Develop* in the viewer):
- **Left:** Presets (built-in: Natural, Vivid, Soft film, B&W, Warm evening, Cool morning; user presets saved with a choice of panels; hovering previews on the canvas) · History (every change; click to step back) · Snapshots (named states). Stacked sections so more can join.
- **Centre:** canvas; before/after (`\`); fit / 100 %; clipping overlay.
- **Right:** histogram (RGB, clipping indicators); **Auto**; panels — **Basic** (white balance temperature/tint with *As shot*, exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, texture, clarity, dehaze, vibrance, saturation), **Tone curve** (point curve, master + R/G/B), **Colour** (HSL, 8 bands × H/S/L), **Colour grading** (shadows/midtones/highlights wheels, blending, balance), **Detail** (sharpening amount/radius/detail/masking; **Denoise** button + strength), **Effects** (post-crop vignette amount/midpoint/feather; grain amount/size). Reset per panel and all; copy/paste settings (choose panels); **Export…**.
- **Top bar** (revised 2026-09-22): the source (library, an album, a search, the selection it was opened with) and its count; **selection** — *Select all*, *Select none*, *Invert*, and one-click *Select picked*, *Select rejected*, *Select by label* (each colour); the **mark filter** (Picked / Rejected / Unflagged, five colour dots, minimum rating) that narrows the filmstrip; sort (capture time, rating, file name); *Sync settings* (paste the current photo's develop onto the selection); **Export selected…**, which opens the export sheet on exactly the selected photos.

**As built (Round 4J, 2026-09-26; plan `2026-09-26-round-4j-develop-workspace.md`):**
- **One header.** The top bar is drawn into the shell's title-bar row (`TitlebarPortal` → `app-shell__toolbar`), beside the window controls and never under them; the gaps stay the window's drag handle, and the row gets a hairline only while a screen draws there. Below 1280 px the mark filter, rating and order fold into a *Filter* menu; below 1000 px the count and the labels of *Sync settings* and *Export…* go, so everything fits at the 900 px minimum.
- **The shell's clothes.** Develop is transparent over the page backdrop (Glass's wash included); the columns and filmstrip use `--bg-sidebar` with the theme's blur; section titles are the sidebar's (small caps, tracked); the stage is `--develop-stage-bg`, set per theme and scheme (text on it ≥ 4.5:1, tested). Each column is its only scroller; the route container around Develop neither scrolls nor keeps a gutter (`.route-transition--fixed`).
- **Zoom** (`routes/develop/zoom.ts`, `zoom-bar.tsx`): fit, fill, or a level from 5 to 400 % (100 % = one image pixel per device pixel), from the bar at the canvas's lower right (compare menu, −, a level menu of 25–400 %, +, *Fit*, *Fill*, *1:1*), `Z` (fit ↔ the last level), a double-click (to the last level at the point), `Ctrl+=` / `Ctrl+−` (steps through the levels above fit), `Ctrl+0` (fit), and Ctrl+wheel or a touchpad pinch around the cursor (`wheelFactor` is exponential, so small deltas add up smoothly). **Amendment (Phase 8e, 2026-10-03; plan `2026-09-26-phase-8e-develop-crop-and-masks.md`): Crop and Masks in Develop.**
- **Crop** is the first entry of the right column's panel list. `R` opens the tool (Lightroom's key): aspect presets (Original, Free, 1:1, 4:5, 3:2, 16:9, custom W:H with a lock), rotate left/right, flip H/V, straighten (±45° slider and a *draw a level line* tool), and the quick editor's thirds/grid guides. While the tool is open the canvas shows the whole photo (the preview is rendered without crop or turns, `geometry: false`) with the crop rectangle over it; *Done* or Enter writes the geometry into the recipe as one history step (*Crop*), Esc puts the old geometry back. A turn or a flip is a step of its own.
- **Masks** follow Crop in the panel list: the photo's masks (name, eye, a small weight map), *New mask ▸ Brush / Linear gradient / Radial gradient*, and per mask *Add ▸* / *Subtract ▸* a shape, *Invert*, *Opacity*, *Duplicate*, *Rename*, *Delete* (with Undo). Under the list sit the selected mask's local sliders — temperature, tint, exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, texture, clarity, dehaze, saturation, sharpness — with Basic's ranges and double-click reset. On the canvas a linear gradient has three lines (start, centre, end) and a rotate handle; a radial one an ellipse with four size handles, a rotate handle and a feather ring; the brush a two-ring cursor (size and feather). `[` / `]` size, `Shift+[` / `Shift+]` feather, Alt erases, Shift draws a straight stroke. `O` shows the weight as a tinted overlay (on while painting), `Shift+O` cycles its colour (a token). A whole stroke, pointer down to up, is one history step (*Brush*).
- **AI masks** (Subject, Sky) are **not** part of 8e. They are Phase 8f, gated on a segmentation model whose code *and* weights allow commercial use, vetted like Phase C.

**Amendment (Phase 8f, 2026-11-02; plan `2026-11-02-phase-8f-ai-masks.md`): AI masks (Lumen Facet).**
- **Julian's decisions:** Sky, Subject, **Background** and Person ship first (Background added with his approval of this amendment, 2026-11-02: "the sky mask detection just be good for skies and the background detection good for backgrounds of subjects"); skin, hair and clothes wait (MediaPipe is not converted). Behind an Experimental switch (*Facet*, like Glyph). A mask stays put when the crop changes. Models trained on non-commercial data (BiRefNet_lite, SAM 2.1) stay out.
- **Model:** EoMT-S COCO panoptic, one run per photo on the display-oriented photo before any edit; measurements in `docs/THIRD-PARTY-MODELS.md`. *Subject* is the most prominent COCO thing (area, centre, score; living things count double), with others of its kind that score at least a quarter as much. *Person* is one choice per person. **Sky** counts only when the model's score for it is at least 0.95, so a plain backdrop behind a portrait isn't sky (measured with the processor's black padding: two portraits' backdrops 0.93 and 0.92, a dark workshop ceiling 0.78; real skies 0.98 and 0.99 in photos with people, 0.99 or more on 49 of 52 without; only three false skies were in the sample, so the bar may need tuning). *(First set at 0.90 from a run that padded with mean grey instead of black; P1's preprocess test caught that.)* **Background** is the inverse of Subject: everything behind the chosen subject, a portrait's backdrop included. A photo with none says so (*No sky found*); a statue or a building is not a COCO thing, so it has no subject and no background.
- **Panel:** *New mask ▸*, *Add ▸* and *Subtract ▸* gain Sky, Subject, Background and Person ▸ Person 1…n. So an AI mask is one shape of a mask and combines with brush and gradients, invert and opacity as any shape does. A shape has *Detect again*. With the switch off or no model, these items are hidden.
- **Recipe:** a new shape `ai { add, kind: sky | subject | background | person, instance, raster: <hash> }`, in the unedited photo's coordinates like every shape, so a crop clips it and turns and flips carry it (8e's rule). A recipe with one is still `version: 3`. *(As built: the field is `target`, because `kind` is `MaskShape`'s serde tag.)*
- **The raster is stored, not recomputed:** table `ai_masks` (migration 0032): asset, hash, kind, instance, model, score, size, 8-bit weights deflated (at most 160 px on the long side; about 2 KB each at the median, 15 KB per photo, measured). It is in the database, not the recipe, because recipes are sent through `invoke` and copied into every history step; and not in a cache, because an export must not change when a cache or a model goes away. A new model version leaves old masks as they were until *Detect again*.
- **Rendering:** Rust samples the raster through `FrameMap` and refines it at render resolution with a guided filter. The guide is the frame's luminance before global Basic, so moving a slider never moves the mask's edge; the radius scales with the frame's long edge, so preview, 1:1 region and export agree. *(As built: a fast guided filter, its coefficients on a coarse grid anchored to the frame, because the plain one holds about eight frame-sized buffers; the radius goes by the unedited photo's long edge in frame pixels, so a crop doesn't change it; a symmetric contrast curve on the output takes off the glow over flat backdrops.)* A missing raster (a mask pasted from another photo) weighs nothing and the panel offers *Detect again*; it is never an error.
- **Overlay:** the webview fetches the raster's bytes from `photos://ai-mask/<asset>/<hash>`, never through `invoke`, and samples it with the same bilinear maths, held by `mask-golden.json`. The overlay shows the unrefined edge; only Rust refines. *(Builder's call, to spare the webview the guide.)*

**Colour labels and flags** (revised 2026-09-22: **Develop only**): labels red/yellow/green/blue/purple; flags Pick/Reject/none. They are a culling tool, so they appear only in Develop — on the filmstrip, in the top bar's filter and selection, in the canvas's corner and in Develop's context menu — never on the library grid, in the viewer or in the library's filters. Keys inside Develop: `P` / `X` / `U`, `Alt+1–5` and `Alt+0`. Exported as `xmp:Label` (the names Lightroom/Bridge use: Red, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple) with `xmp:Rating`; flags have no standard field and stay in Lumen.

## 2. Data (approved)

Migration **0016**: `assets.color_label INTEGER` (0 none, 1–5), `assets.flag INTEGER` (0 none, 1 pick, −1 reject), indexes for the filters; `develop_presets(id, name, settings_json, panels_json, builtin, created_at)`; `edit_history(asset_id, seq, recipe_json, label, snapshot_name, created_at)` capped at 200 steps per asset, snapshots uncapped.

Recipe **v2** (`photos-core::edits`), still one per asset in `edits`, JSON:

```
{ version: 2,
  geometry: { crop, rotate, straighten, flip_h, flip_v },
  develop:  null | { basic{…}, curve{master[], r[], g[], b[]}, hsl[8]{h,s,l}, grading{shadows, midtones, highlights, blending, balance}, detail{sharpen{amount,radius,detail,masking}, denoise{strength, model}|null}, effects{vignette{…}, grain{…}}, auto: bool },
  trim:     null | { segments: [{start_ms, end_ms}] } }
```

**As built:** the geometry fields stay flat on the recipe (`crop`, `rotate`, `straighten`, `flip_h`, and the new `flip_v`) rather than nesting under `geometry` — the layout, the renderer, the edit session and every stored recipe already use them — and `develop` and `trim` are added beside them. A v1 recipe parses and upgrades: its crop, rotate, straighten and flip stay where they are; exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, tint, highlights and shadows become `develop.basic`. `output_size`, the edit-version high-water mark and the `v=` URL scheme are unchanged.

**Recipe v3 (amendment, Phase 8e):** `develop.masks` — a list of masks, each `{ id, name, shapes[], invert, opacity 0–1, adjust{…}, visible }`. It lives **inside `develop`**, not beside it, so everything that already treats develop as one thing treats masks the same way: the before view (develop removed, geometry kept) shows no masks, a RAW with masks renders from sensor data, and copy/paste carries them as a panel (*Masks*, off by default). Presets **never** carry masks (they are photo-specific): saving a preset drops the *Masks* panel and the masks themselves.
- Shapes, combined in order: `brush { add, radius (of the photo's long edge), feather 0–1, flow 0–1, points[[u16, u16]] }` with points quantised to 0–4096 of the photo, `linear { add, from, to }` (full effect at `from`, none at `to`) and `radial { add, center, radius[rx, ry], angle (degrees), feather 0–1, inside }`.
- **Coordinates are the unedited photo's**: 0–1 of the display-oriented photo before any crop, turn, flip or straighten. Changing the crop never moves a mask; a mask outside the new crop is simply clipped; turns and flips carry it with the photo.
- `adjust` (`LocalAdjust`): temperature, tint (±100), exposure (±5 EV), contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, texture, clarity, dehaze, saturation, sharpness (±1).
- A recipe is written as `version: 3` only when it has a mask; any other recipe stays `version: 2` and serialises byte for byte as before (`masks` is left out when empty). A mask with all-zero `adjust`, or hidden, changes no pixel (`changes_pixels`), but it is kept: an empty mask is the user's work in progress.
- A 2,000-dab brush mask stays under 64 KB of JSON; recipes live in `edits` and in every history snapshot.

## 3. The develop pipeline (approach A: Rust, CPU)

One implementation, in `photos-media::develop`, used by the live preview (at screen resolution) and the export (full resolution), in **linear light, f32, Rec.2020 primaries** internally:

1. **Source.** RAW: `rawler` decodes the sensor data; black/white levels; **as-shot white balance** (or the recipe's temperature/tint converted to channel multipliers through the camera matrix); demosaic (rawler's; Bayer, and X-Trans where rawler supports it — otherwise the embedded preview with a notice); camera → XYZ → working space. Non-RAW: decode, apply its ICC/colour space (sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB), linearise.
2. **Denoise base** (8d): when the recipe has denoise, the source is replaced by the cached denoised base blended by strength.
3. **Geometry** first (crop, rotate, straighten, flip), so every later stage works on the pixels that will be seen.
4. **Basic:** exposure (EV) and white balance (non-RAW: a von Kries adaptation in linear light); highlights/shadows/whites/blacks as luminance-masked tone adjustments; contrast as an S-curve about middle grey; texture and clarity as local contrast (a blurred-luminance detail layer at two radii, scaled to the image, so preview and export agree); dehaze (dark-channel estimate); vibrance (saturation weighted toward the less saturated) and saturation.
5. **Tone curve:** monotone cubic (Fritsch–Carlson) through the points, on a perceptual encoding; master then per channel.
6. **HSL:** eight overlapping hue bands, smooth weights, in an OKLCh-like perceptual space; **colour grading** as luminance-weighted tints.
7. **Detail:** unsharp-mask sharpening on luminance with an edge mask (`masking`).
8. **Effects:** post-crop vignette; grain (seeded by the asset id, so exports are reproducible).
9. **Output:** to sRGB or Display P3 with its ICC profile (generated in code: matrix/TRC v2 profiles); 8- or 16-bit; for display, sRGB.

**Preview speed:** the decoded, geometry-free linear base is cached per asset at the preview size (≤ 2048 px long edge) in memory; a slider move re-runs stages 3–9 only, on the preview-sized image, off the main thread, latest-wins. Target ≤ 120 ms for a 2048 px preview on the dev machine (release build), measured; full-resolution export ≤ 3 s for 24 MP without denoise.

**Histogram** is computed from the rendered preview (256 bins × RGB + luminance) and returned with it.

**As built (Phase 8c, 2026-09-23):**
- **Working space:** linear **Rec.709/sRGB** primaries, f32, unclamped, not Rec.2020. Nothing clips before output, and OKLab (HSL, grading) is defined on exactly these primaries. **White balance** is a luminance-preserving von Kries gain in that space for every source, RAW included, on top of the camera's as-shot multipliers. Rawler applies its multipliers before its matrix, so honouring temperature through the camera matrix would mean caching camera-space data per photo. *As shot* means temperature and tint 0.
- **RAW** (`develop::raw`): rawler 0.8's `RawDevelop` with every step but `SRgb` gives linear sRGB, clipped to 0–1 by its `clip_euclidean_norm_avg`, and the result is oriented like the embedded preview. Sensor data is scene-linear, a stop darker and flatter than a camera JPEG, so Lumen adds a **base look** as part of the source (as a camera profile is): a 1.5 EV baseline with a Reinhard shoulder to that white, then contrast 0.4 and vibrance 0.45. It was tuned on a CC0 Nikon Z 6II file from raw.pixls.us to the camera JPEG's mean brightness (+0.03 stops). A 24 MP decode takes 0.57 s. **The RAW rule:** only a recipe with `develop` renders from sensor data; unedited and geometry-only RAW keep the embedded preview, so a crop never changes a photo's colours. Develop itself always reads the sensor.
- **Stages** (`develop::process`): geometry; Basic gains (white balance, exposure); texture and clarity (the detail layer of log luminance at σ = 0.0015 and 0.012 of the long edge, clarity weighted to the midtones and blurred on a σ/4-coarse grid); dehaze (dark channel on a 1 %-of-the-long-edge grid, airlight from the haziest 1 % of cells); the four tone ranges as Gaussian bells over stops about 0.18, then contrast as `1.2·c·tanh(s/1.5)` stops, both applied as a luminance ratio so hue holds; vibrance and saturation; the curve (Fritsch–Carlson on the sRGB-encoded value, through a linear-in, linear-out table spaced by square root); HSL and grading in one OKLab pass (bands centred at OKLCh 25, 60, 100, 140, 195, 262, 300, 330°, raised cosines of 45° half-width, neutrals untouched); sharpening (an unsharp mask with a halo limit and a gradient mask, radius in full-resolution pixels); vignette; grain (value noise seeded by the content hash). Radii of texture, clarity and dehaze scale with the rendered frame; sharpening and grain scale by `Ctx::scale`. A 3000 px render downsized matches the 1000 px preview at ≥ 32 dB.
- **Preview** (`src-tauri/src/develop_preview.rs`): bases of 2048 px (up to 4096 for a tight crop), two photos kept, decoded straight from 8-bit into the downscale. Requests carry a counter; a render overtaken by a newer one stops before it encodes. Measured, release: **93 ms** for a 2048 px preview with every stage on (budget 120), **1.00 s** for a 24 MP render. The vertical blur pass is a running sum over strips of 64 columns, read row by row; transposing the image twice had cost 16 ms of texture and clarity. **100 %** renders the visible part of the full-size frame from one kept slot. `Ctx` carries the part's place in the frame and the frame's airlight, so the part matches the whole (≥ 35 dB), and the slot is freed on zooming out.
- **Histogram** is square-root scaled for display: a log scale made every shape look full.
- **Mask stage (amendment, Phase 8e).** After the global Basic stages (gains, texture/clarity, dehaze, tone ranges and colour) and **before the curve**, each visible mask with a non-zero `adjust` is applied: `develop::masks::rasterize` turns the mask into weights `m` (0–1) at the render's resolution — the shapes are mapped from the unedited photo into the rendered frame by a `FrameMap` (the render's own geometry: flips, turns, straighten, crop, and for the 100 % view the region's place) — then its `LocalAdjust` runs on a copy of the mask's bounding box through **the same** Basic, texture/clarity, dehaze and saturation code as the global stage, and `out = lerp(in, adjusted, m)`. Local sharpness adds `m · sharpness` to the global sharpening amount. Brush: dabs every `radius/4` along each stroke, each a smoothstep disc (full inside `radius·(1−feather)`), combined `1 − Π(1 − flow·w)`; an erase stroke multiplies by `1 − w`. Linear: a smoothstep along the projection from `from` to `to`. Radial: an ellipse with a smoothstep feather, inside or outside. A subtracted shape multiplies by `1 − w`; then `invert`, then `opacity`. The feather is clamped at the frame's edge, never wrapped. Because every render goes through `develop::process`, a mask looks the same in the preview, the 100 % view, derivatives, slideshow stills and exports.
- **Output** stays 8b's sRGB 8-bit. The quick editor's old renderer is gone: every render (derivatives, exports, the quick editor's previews) goes through `develop`.

**As built (Phase 8e, 2026-10-03; plan `2026-09-26-phase-8e-develop-crop-and-masks.md`):**
- **Recipe v3** (`photos-core::edits`): `Develop.masks: Vec<Mask>`, left out of the JSON when empty, so a v2 recipe writes back byte for byte (golden file `photos-core/src/testdata/recipe-v2.json`). `version` is 3 only when there is a mask. `Mask::normalized` clamps like every other field (≤ 64 masks, ≤ 500 shapes, ≤ 20,000 points a stroke, names ≤ 64 characters); a brush with no points is dropped. `Mask::is_effective` (shown, opacity > 0, an adjustment) decides `changes_pixels`; an empty mask is still saved. 2,000 dabs are well under 64 KB of JSON (tested). `Panel::Masks` merges only masks; `save_preset` drops the panel and the masks.
- **Rendering** (`photos-media::develop::masks`): `FrameMap` is the render's own geometry as one affine map (flips, quarter turns, the inverse of `straighten_source`, the crop's rounded offset, and for the 100 % view the region's corner), carried in `Ctx::frame_map` and set by `process`; the 100 % view builds it from the full-size base and caches it with its frame. `rasterize` runs row by row in parallel (each brush row looks only at the dabs within a radius of it). The local Basic reuses `basic::gains_of` and `basic::Tones` per pixel, in place and only where the weight is above 0, for masks without texture, clarity or dehaze; with those, a copy of the mask's box padded by 3σ of clarity goes through `local::texture_clarity` / `local::dehaze` with `Ctx::region` set, so radii are the frame's. Local dehaze uses `Ctx::airlight` when given, else the image's at the mask stage. Local sharpness adds per pixel to the amount (`detail::sharpen_with`, clamped −1…2.5).
- **Measured** (release, `develop_speed`): every stage plus three masks (a 200-dab brush, a linear and a radial gradient) **143–146 ms** at 2048 px (the every-stage baseline was 97–101 ms that day) and **1.32–1.34 s** at 24 MP. The first version took 190 ms / 1.81 s; rasterising in parallel took the three masks from 41 ms to 8 ms.
- **The webview** draws masks with the same maths (`src/lib/mask-raster.ts`, held to `src/lib/mask-golden.json` within 1/255, which a Rust test writes) and maps every pointer back into the unedited photo (`fromFrame`). The crop tool shares `components/crop-tool` with the viewer; while it is open the session sends the recipe without geometry (`withoutGeometry`) and the CSS-transform canvas lays the geometry out, so no Rust flag was needed.
- **Export** (`metadata_transfer::every_format_carries_a_masked_photo`, ExifTool 13.59 / FFmpeg 9.0.1): a turned, cropped photo with three masks against its preview: JPEG 51.7 dB, PNG 65.3, WebP 50.8, AVIF 47.2, HEIC 46.5 (bar 32), metadata carried and verified in all five.
- **Painting in WebView2** (dev build, CDP, 5 s at 60 Hz on a 3093 × 1861 photo): frame p95 33 ms, worst 50 ms; a preview every 107 ms (median) during the stroke and 141 ms after release. The overlay paints the stroke itself (`StrokeTint`, dirty-box repaints) and the session hears of it every 100 ms.
- **History:** a crop's drags are one *Crop* step on *Done*; turns, flips, straighten and each brush stroke (pointer down to up) are steps of their own; Esc drops only what changed since the last step. While painting, the preview loop's one-in-flight, newest-waiting rule holds (60 moves → one request out, then the whole stroke).

## 4. ML (approaches chosen)

**Auto** (`photos-core::develop_auto` for the maths, `photos-ml` for the scoring):
1. *Measure* on a 256 px render of the unadjusted base: luminance histogram → exposure so the median sits near middle grey (bounded), whites/blacks so ≤ 0.1 % clips, highlights/shadows from the tails; grey-world white balance over mid-tones, excluding clipped and highly saturated pixels.
2. *Understand*: the CLIP embedding against scene prototypes (`scene:sunset`, `scene:night`, `scene:snow`, `scene:beach`, `scene:backlit`, `scene:portrait`, `scene:foggy`, `scene:indoor`) adjusts the prior — keep a sunset warm and a night dark, let snow be bright, don't neutralise candlelight.
3. *Search*: from the prior, three rounds of 16 candidates (perturbations of exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, temperature, tint, vibrance, saturation; shrinking steps; seeded) rendered at 224 px, embedded, scored by `aesthetic − λ·distance_from_prior − μ·clipping`; the best becomes the recipe's `basic`, flagged `auto`. Deterministic; ≈ 50 embeddings (~2 s). With no model present, step 1 alone runs (and says so).

**Denoise**: a pretrained restoration network, vetted like Phase 7 (licence of code *and* weights must allow commercial use; candidates NAFNet-SIDD and SCUNet-real; chosen on quality and CPU/GPU speed, recorded in `THIRD-PARTY-MODELS.md`), exported to ONNX fp16, fetched with the other models. Run **on demand** from the Detail panel with progress, tiled with overlap, **DirectML on Windows** when available (the prebuilt ONNX Runtime ships `DirectML.dll`), CPU otherwise. The output is the cached **denoised base** (`derivatives/denoise/{hash}_{model}.exr`-like linear f16 file), rebuilt whenever missing; the recipe stores only `{strength, model}`. For RAW it runs on the demosaiced linear image.

**As built (Phase 8d, 2026-09-26; plan `2026-09-25-phase-8d-auto-and-denoise.md`):**
- **Auto** (`photos-core::develop_auto`, `src-tauri/src/develop_auto.rs`): measures the photo's own crop at 256 px (percentiles 0.1 / 50 / 99.9, shares below 0.01 and above 0.9, IQR in stops). Grey world runs over pixels with luminance 0.05–0.8, max channel < 0.98 and chroma < 0.5. It is applied at **half strength**, within ±60 temperature and ±25 tint: at full strength it pushed tint to +77 on foliage.
- **Scenes** are read at softmax temperature **0.01** (CLIP's own logit scale; at 0.05 no scene ever passed the 0.25 floor, and night photos got +2 EV). The search is 1 + 3 × 16 candidates, SplitMix64-seeded from the content hash, λ = 0.02, μ = 2. It keeps every other panel and never clips more than the prior.
- **Auto measured on 20 of Julian's photos (copies):** aesthetic 0.406 → 0.433 on average (best +0.084), never below the prior beyond noise, ≤ 2.7 s each. Without the image model it returns the measured prior and says so. UI: *Auto* above the panels, `Mod+U`, palette *Auto tone*.
- **Denoise model:** NAFNet width 32 trained on SIDD (MIT code, MIT data), 59 MB fp16 ONNX. SCUNet segfaults under DirectML and is 3.6× slower on the CPU (`docs/THIRD-PARTY-MODELS.md`).
- **Tiling:** 512 px tiles overlapping by 32, padded by reflection to multiples of 16, blended with raised-cosine weights. Tiled vs one-tile differences are 0.0058 along the seams and 0.0055 elsewhere, so there is no seam.
- **DirectML** links through `ort`'s `directml` feature and Windows' own `DirectML.dll`. A GPU failure on the first tile moves the run to the CPU once. CPU and GPU agree within 3.1/255 at the 99th percentile (DirectML computes in fp16).
- **Denoise speed:** a 24 MP photo in **9.1 s on DirectML** (RTX 3080 Ti) and **4.6 min on the CPU**, beyond the hoped-for ~60 s. It is a background job with progress and Cancel.
- **Spec amendment:** the denoised base is a **16-bit sRGB PNG** (`derivatives/denoise/{hash[..2]}/{hash}_{model}.png`, written through `.part`), not a linear f16 file. The base is already clipped to 0–1, 16-bit sRGB keeps ≥ 12 bits in the shadows, and `Linear::from_dynamic` reads it exactly.
- **Stage 2** (`develop::blend_denoised`) applies wherever a base is loaded: the preview (once per base), the 100 % view (its frame keyed by denoise), edited derivatives and exports. A missing base renders without it; an export builds it first when the model is there.

## 5. Export and metadata (ExifTool)

**Sidecar.** ExifTool (Phil Harvey; Artistic/GPL, run as a separate program) is bundled like FFmpeg — `scripts/fetch-exiftool.ps1|sh` into `src-tauri/binaries/`, resolved the same way (env `LUMEN_EXIFTOOL_DIR`, next to the exe, `src-tauri/binaries`, `PATH`), run in `-stay_open` mode so a batch pays Perl's start-up once. **Missing ExifTool is a state:** export still works, and the dialog says metadata cannot be carried until it is installed — it never silently drops it.

**Pixels.** JPEG (quality, 4:4:4 above 90), PNG (8/16-bit), **WebP** (lossy with quality, or lossless — the `webp` crate), **AVIF** (FFmpeg `libaom-av1`, 8/10-bit, `avif` muxer), **HEIC** (FFmpeg `libx265` encodes 512 × 512 tiles as HEVC; a HEIF writer of Lumen's own assembles them as a `grid` image — `ftyp heic`, `meta` with `pitm`, `iinf`, `iloc`, `iref dimg`, `iprp` with `hvcC`/`ispe`/`colr`/`irot`-free — the layout iPhones write), *Original* (the file, byte for byte). Colour space sRGB or Display P3, profile embedded in every format.

**Metadata, per export:**
1. `-TagsFromFile <original> -all:all -unsafe` — EXIF (including maker notes), XMP (every namespace), IPTC, GPS, QuickTime keys for video; ExifTool translates between families where the target lacks one (e.g. IPTC → XMP for PNG/WebP/AVIF/HEIC; EXIF into PNG `eXIf`).
2. Then corrections, because the pixels changed: `Orientation=1` (rotation is baked in); pixel dimensions (`ExifImageWidth/Height`, `ImageWidth/Height` where present) set to the output; the embedded **thumbnail/preview removed** (it would show the unedited photo); the **ICC profile replaced** by the output's (the source's no longer describes the pixels); `Software`/`CreatorTool` = Lumen; `ModifyDate` = now; `DateTimeOriginal`, `CreateDate`, offsets and sub-seconds untouched.
3. Lumen's own facts added: `xmp:Rating`, `xmp:Label` (colour), `dc:title`/`dc:description`/`dc:subject` from the catalogue's title/caption/keywords (Phase 4b extras), people's names as `MWG-RS` regions are **not** written (faces are Lumen's, not the photo's).
4. **Verification:** read back a fixed set — capture date/time and offset, GPS, make/model/serial, lens, exposure triangle, focal length, rating, label, title/keywords — from source and output; any difference fails that file's export (the file is kept, flagged) and the result lists what did not survive, per file.

**Video:** trims export through FFmpeg — precise (re-encode H.264/HEVC) by default, or *fast* (stream copy, cut at key frames) — with `-map_metadata 0 -movflags use_metadata_tags`, then ExifTool copies QuickTime/XMP/GPS (`©xyz`, `com.apple.quicktime.location.ISO6709`, creation date) and sets duration-dependent tags from the output; verified like photos.

**Destination** (revised 2026-09-22): a folder the user picks, or **Add to library** — the export is written to a temporary folder inside the library and ingested like an import (`FilesSource`), so it becomes a new photo beside the original with the same capture date, place and metadata, `capture_source` read from its own EXIF. Its catalogue row records `derived_from` (the original's id) and the recipe is *not* carried over (the pixels already contain it). A duplicate (same content hash) is reported, not added twice. The export sheet offers both from every surface — the viewer, a selection, and Develop's *Export selected…*.

**Safety:** output names never collide (`name.jpg`, `name (2).jpg`); writes go to a temporary file in the destination and are renamed on success; ExifTool is never pointed at an original (its argument list is built from typed paths, and the original is only ever the `-TagsFromFile` source); `-overwrite_original` applies only to Lumen's temporary output.

**As built (Phase 8b, 2026-09-23; ExifTool 13.59, FFmpeg 9.0.1 gyan essentials):**
- **No `-unsafe`.** It would carry a RAW's IFD0 layout tags (ImageWidth, Compression, strip offsets) into a file they do not describe. `-all:all` without it already brings maker notes, GPS, IPTC and every XMP namespace. Lumen's own IPTC → XMP table (`metadata_transfer::IPTC_TO_XMP`) runs *before* `-all:all` for targets without IPTC, so a real XMP value in the original wins; the Windows package ships no `arg_files/`.
- **Colour is sRGB only** for now (no Display P3 choice) and PNG is 8-bit. JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIC embed Lumen's own sRGB profile (`icc::srgb()`, "sRGB IEC61966-2.1 (Lumen)"). **ExifTool silently skips `ICC_Profile` in AVIF**, so AVIF declares its colour with `colr nclx` from FFmpeg's `setparams` (the plain `-color_*` options were dropped by the filter graph); Lumen's HEIF writer writes both `nclx` and `prof`.
- **Verification** reads fixed facts from both files with `-j -n -G1` and compares IPTC-or-XMP either way, numbers to a millionth, and lists as sets. It also checks the corrections (Orientation 1, output sizes, our profile). A difference does **not** fail the file: it is written and listed in `export://finished`'s `losses`, and the toast offers *Details*. A failed ExifTool run is reported as a loss named `Metadata`. The round-trip test first verifies a *bare* export and requires it to lack the facts, so an empty answer means something.
- **Temporary file:** `.lumen-part-<final name>`, hidden and beside it. It keeps the extension because ExifTool picks its writer by type. The final name is reserved (an empty file) when the batch starts.
- **Pixels come from the source**, not from the cached quality-90 `full` JPEG, so a PNG or lossless WebP of an edit is lossless.
- **Video.** *Precise* is `trim`/`atrim` + `concat`, H.264 CRF 18 (HEVC CRF 20 with `hvc1` for an HEVC source, 10-bit kept), AAC 192k. Decoding applies the rotation, so the output is upright with none. *Fast* goes through the concat demuxer with `inpoint`/`outpoint` and stream copy, which keeps the rotation; it is offered for QuickTime/MP4 only and falls back to precise otherwise. **Neither keeps the container's tags** (the concat demuxer has none), and `-TagsFromFile <original> -all:all` restores QuickTime's creation date and the `Keys` location, make and model without copying `Rotation`. Measured: two segments of a 6 s clip give 4.000 s precise and 4.07 s fast (key frames every second). A QuickTime source stays `.mov`, everything else becomes `.mp4`, named `<stem>-trimmed.<ext>`. No duration-dependent tags are set: the muxer writes them.
- **Add to library** writes into `<library>/tmp/export-<batch>/`, imports with `FilesSource` through the normal `spawn_import`, then links each new asset by **content hash** (`photos_import::content_hash`, the same BLAKE3) with `assets::link_derived` (migration 0017). A drop guard on the import's `after` callback removes the folder and sends `export://finished` however the import ends. The viewer's info panel shows *Exported from <original>*.
- Catalogue keywords are now stored one per line, so a two-word keyword stays one on export. Older rows are still read a word at a time.

## 6. Testing

- Pipeline: pure functions per stage with hand-computed values (identity recipe is identity within 1/255; exposure +1 EV doubles linear values; curve through identity points is identity; HSL band weights sum to 1; vignette symmetric); golden 64 px renders for each panel; preview vs export agree within tolerance at the same size.
- RAW: an `#[ignore]` test on real NEF/DNG/CR3 files (`LUMEN_RAW_FOLDER`) — decodes, renders, and as-shot WB within tolerance of the embedded preview's colours.
- Metadata: **round-trip tests per format** on fixtures carrying EXIF+GPS+maker notes+IPTC+XMP+ICC (a JPEG, a DNG, an iPhone HEIC, a PNG with XMP): export to JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and HEIC, read back with ExifTool, assert the verification set equal and Orientation/dimensions/ICC corrected. Video: a trimmed MOV keeps creation date and location.
- HEIC writer: the output decodes with FFmpeg to the input within tolerance, and ExifTool reads its metadata.
- Auto: deterministic on a fixture; improves the aesthetic score over the prior; never clips more than the prior. Denoise: PSNR gain on a synthetically noised fixture; tiles seamless (no seam above tolerance).
- Safety: a test that exports over an existing name creates `name (2)`; a failed encode leaves no partial file; originals' bytes and mtimes are unchanged after every export test.
